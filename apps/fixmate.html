Say what's happening. The app works out the rest

You don't need to know the name of anything to start. Five steps from a vague description to a finished repair, or an honest referral.

Describe it in your own words

Water under the sink. Light flickers. Door won't shut. Whatever you would say to a friend on the phone is enough to start.

Answer three questions

Short, specific questions that narrow it down — where, when, and what changed. No terminology, no diagrams to interpret.

Find out whose job it is

The app tells you straight: this is a do-it-yourself repair, this needs a licensed trade, or this needs turning off and a phone call right now.

Follow the steps

If it's yours, you get the sequence with the tools you already own, photographs of the actual parts, and what it should look like when it's right.

Know when to stop

Every guide has a stop point. If you hit it, the app says so and tells you what to say when you ring the trade.

Questions

Is it safe for me to do this myself?

Every guide starts by answering that. FixMate separates repairs into what anyone can do, what needs care, and what's licensed work — and it won't walk you through licensed work no matter how you ask.

Does it cover electrical?

Only what's safe to know: resetting a tripped safety switch and finding what caused it, changing globes, understanding your switchboard. Anything behind a face plate is a licensed electrician, and the app says so.

I am renting. What am I allowed to fix?

The app explains the usual split between tenant and landlord responsibility in Australian tenancies, and helps you write the repair request when it's not yours to do.

Do I need a shed full of tools?

No. Guides are written around a basic kit, and each one lists what it needs up front — including when the sensible answer is to hire the tool for a day.

When can I use it?

FixMate is in build. Early access opens to a limited group first, with pricing announced before anyone is charged.
