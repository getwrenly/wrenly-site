A forum answer from 2011 and a video that skips the bit you needed

Doing a trade task for the first time isn't hard because the work is hard. It is hard because nobody tells you the order, the tolerance, or whether you're allowed.

You can find out how to lay a course of bricks. Finding out what has to be done before you lay it's the actual problem.

Questions

What can I legally do myself?

BuildMate answers this before it shows you a method. Electrical work, most plumbing and drainage, and gas work must be done by a licensed trade in every Australian state. Structural work often needs certification. The app states the rule that applies where you are, and says plainly when to stop.

Does this replace a licensed tradesperson?

No. It is a reference and a teaching tool. For licensed work it explains what's happening and what good work looks like so you can hold your trade to it — it doesn't walk you through doing it yourself.

Which standards does it use?

The relevant AS and AS/NZS standards and the National Construction Code are referenced by clause where they apply. The app doesn't reproduce them, and it tells you when a job genuinely requires the standard itself or an engineer.

Is it written for Australia?

Yes. Timber sizes, treatment classes, climate zones, wind ratings, terminology and licensing are all Australian, state by state.
